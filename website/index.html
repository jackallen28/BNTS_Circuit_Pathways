PiPath - Welcome✕

PiPath

Python on the Raspberry Pi, for Years 7 to 10. Students build real circuits: traffic lights, reaction games, a parking sensor, a photo booth, even a machine that learns.

24 activities · Simulator and real Pi · runs offline on a Pi 3B · everything on this page is in the release version

Follow Mia, a new student, through one project: Traffic Lights.

1

Hello, who's working on this Pi?

A profile is just a first name and the school. No surnames, emails or passwords. Students who share a Pi can add up to four names.

Then each student chooses how much help they want. Tap one to see what it means:

2

Everything is open

No levels to unlock. Students pick any of the 24 activities, in any order. Free Play is always first, for students who want to build their own thing.

SimpleModeratePi & ComputerAdvancedElectronics LabCamera & AI

3

Wire it, then check it

The Wiring + Safety card shows where every wire goes, with the Pi's 40-pin map. Blue HAT off before changing wires!

Check my wiring runs a test program: each LED blinks in turn, then it asks for a button press, and every part gets a ✓.

4

Your turn: Traffic Lights, step 1

This is the real first step, shrunk to fit. Press Run first (go on, even with the blank), meet the Coach, then fill in the blank. Try changing the help level too.

5

Errors are clues, not failures

PiPath's Coach knows the common mistakes, and explains each one kindly. If a student is stuck, it notices and offers a nudge:

Tap a card to flip it. Nudges are offers, never forced, and each shows once per step.

6

See it, then do it

Watch the goal plays the step's answer on the on-screen board, so students know what they're aiming for. Their own code isn't touched.

On a real Pi, switch Run on: Real Pi and the same code lights the LEDs on the breadboard.

7

Stars for independence

Finish an activity and earn up to ★★★. Stars count the hints you used, not how fast you were: they reward working it out yourself.

8

ArduinoPath✕

ArduinoPath

Arduino and ESP coding for Years 7 to 10, in blocks or real C++. Every project starts with the Wiring Wizard, and a test that checks the circuit before any coding.

23 projects · Uno, Nano, NodeMCU, ESP32 · Simulator and real boards · everything on this page is in the release version

Follow Mia through the Smart Bin: a lid that opens when a hand comes close.

1

Pick your help and your board

Like PiPath: a first name and the school, then Beginner, Moderate or Advanced.

Then the board on the desk, and one big choice: type real Arduino code, or snap together Blocks.

2

The Wiring Wizard: meet the parts

Before any code, the wizard introduces each part. Students press Next for each one, or skip ahead.

Then it walks through the wiring one connection at a time, on a breadboard picture and a schematic.

3

Wire it, step by step

The new wire is highlighted, the rest fade back. Flip to the schematic to see the same circuit as a diagram.

The wizard thinks of everything! For example, if a student is using a 3.3V ESP board, the wizard adds the extra resistors that keep the sensor's 5V signal safe, with a ⚠ note.

4

Your turn: try testing a distance sensor example!

Coding only unlocks when the wiring passes its test. Try the real test for the ultrasonic sensor: move your hand towards the sensor and away.

5

Test before you code

Each part takes a turn: LEDs blink, servos sweep, sensors show live numbers. Sensors tick themselves when the reading changes; for lights and motors, students tick I saw it work.

If a part fails, Fix it lists the most likely causes for that part, like Trig and Echo swapped.

6

Blocks that open up Arduino

If your students have used MakeCode on the micro:bit, they already know how this works: colourful drawers, blocks that snap together, and the real code underneath.

Typed Arduino C++ is full of brackets and semicolons that stop beginners in their tracks. With blocks, a Year 7 can build a real Arduino project in their first lesson, then switch to typing the same code when they're ready.

7

Watch the numbers

The Serial Monitor shows the distance as it changes. Drag the hand in the Simulator, or use a real hand on a real board.

Each step has a checklist that ticks itself, and hints that unlock as the student needs them.

8

Watch the goal, then build it

Watch the goal plays the model answer on the board. Then the lid opens for a hand, all by itself.

9

One project, many boards

The same Smart Bin runs on every one of these. Students pick the board on their desk, and ArduinoPath does the rest.

10

Here's what other activities are in the app

From blinking lights to WiFi projects you control from a phone.
